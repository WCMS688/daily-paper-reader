Abstract —Power Delivery Networks (PDNs) are critical com-ponents of modern VLSI chips, providing stable voltage levels while satisfying electromigration (EM) and IR-drop constraints. Conventional PDN design methodologies typically rely on worst-case assumptions, often resulting in over-provisioned networks and inefficient use of resources. This paper presents a rein-forcement learning-based framework for the optimization of workload-aware PDNs. The proposed methodology first generates workload-aware PDNs using architectural power traces obtained from system-level simulations. These power traces are mapped to spatial power density distributions, enabling adaptive allocation of PDN resources according to local current demand. A rein-forcement learning agent then performs wire-width optimization to minimize PDN area while maintaining EM and voltage in-tegrity constraints. Electrical and reliability metrics are obtained using SPICE-based circuit analysis and EM lifetime estimation. Experimental evaluation is performed on a dataset of workload-aware PDNs generated from 4-, 8-, and 16-core multiprocessor floorplans using PARSEC and SPLASH-2 benchmark work-loads. Furthermore, the proposed Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based optimizer reduces the average normalized PDN area by 47% while satisfying all EM and IR-drop constraints. Compared to simulated annealing, the proposed approach achieves comparable optimization quality while providing approximately 26 × faster optimization. 

Index Terms —Power Delivery Networks, Reinforcement Learning, EM/IR 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The increasing integration density of modern multicore systems has significantly intensified the challenges associated with on-chip power delivery [1]. Emerging technologies such as backside power delivery networks (BS-PDNs) further high-light the importance of efficient power distribution by enabling improved routing flexibility, reduced IR drop, and better utilization of routing resources [2]. However, regardless of the underlying PDN architecture, designers must still balance competing objectives including metal area, power integrity, and reliability. Traditional PDN design methodologies are typically based on conservative worst-case assumptions, often resulting in over-provisioned networks that consume excessive routing resources and chip area [3]. At the same time, elec-tromigration (EM) and IR-drop constraints limit the ability to aggressively reduce PDN resources, creating a complex optimization problem for modern VLSI systems. To this end, several methodologies have been proposed for PDN optimization under area, reliability, and voltage integrity

constraints. Early approaches relied on linear and nonlinear programming techniques to minimize routing resources while satisfying EM/IR requirements [4]. More recently, physics-based EM models have enabled more accurate reliability-aware optimization [5], leading to power-grid repair and wire-sizing methodologies based on mathematical optimization, metaheuristics such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) [6], and gradient-based approaches including L-BFGS [7]. In parallel, workload-aware PDN design methodologies have emerged, exploiting architectural power traces to allocate resources according to realistic current demand distributions rather than worst-case assumptions. Despite these advances, existing approaches largely depend on predefined optimization strategies and repeated design-space exploration. The use of reinforcement learning for workload-aware PDN optimization remains largely unexplored, particularly for learning adaptive wire-sizing policies that minimize routing resources while satisfying EM and IR-drop constraints. In this paper, we present a reinforcement learning-based framework for the optimization of workload-aware PDNs. The main contributions of this work are threefold. First , we formulate workload-aware PDN optimization as a reinforce-ment learning problem, enabling an agent to automatically learn wire-sizing strategies from interaction with the design environment. Second , we integrate workload-dependent power distributions derived from architectural simulations into the optimization process, allowing PDN resources to be allocated according to realistic current demands rather than worst-case assumptions. Finally, we evaluate the proposed approach on workload-aware PDNs generated from multicore processor floorplans using representative benchmark applications and compare its performance against simulated annealing. Exper-imental results show that the proposed DQN-based optimizer achieves near-optimal solutions while requiring significantly lower computational cost than a simulated annealing approach. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Sec-tion II presents the background for PDN design. Section III describes the proposed reinforcement learning-based optimiza-tion framework. Experimental setup and results are presented in Section IV, followed by conclusions in Section V. II. B ACKGROUND 

A. Workload-Aware PDN Design 

PDNs are traditionally designed using uniform or worst-case power assumptions to guarantee reliable operation under all possible operating conditions. resources are allocated conservatively across the entire chip, resulting in regular power grid structures that are often over-provisioned [8]. While such designs provide sufficient voltage integrity and EM robustness, they may consume excessive metal resources and reduce the routing capacity available for signal interconnects. In modern multicore systems, power consumption is highly dependent on workload behavior and varies significantly across architectural components. Workload-aware PDN design seeks to exploit this information by incorporating power estimates obtained from architectural simulations into the PDN planning process. Com-pared to conventional worst-case methodologies, workload-aware PDNs can reduce overall metal usage while maintaining voltage integrity and reliability constraints, providing a more efficient starting point for subsequent optimization. 

B. Electromigration in Power Delivery Networks 

EM is a major reliability concern in modern PDNs. It is caused by the momentum transfer from conducting electrons to metal atoms, leading to gradual material transport within interconnects. Over time, this process can result in void formation, increased resistance, and ultimately open-circuit failures. As technology nodes continue to scale and current densities increase, accurate EM assessment has become an important consideration in PDN optimization. One of the earliest and most widely used EM lifetime models is Black’s equation [9], which estimates the mean time to failure (MTTF) of a conductor as: 

MTTF = AJ −ne Ea kT (3) where J is the current density, T is the operating temper-ature, Ea is the activation energy, k is Boltzmann’s constant, and A and n are empirical fitting parameters. Black’s equa-tion provides a simple relationship between current density and expected lifetime, making it attractive for fast reliability estimation. However, it treats each interconnect segment inde-pendently and does not account for stress interactions between neighboring wires in a power grid. To address these limitations, a physics-based stress evolu-tion model was proposed in [10], which describes the temporal evolution of hydrostatic stress within a conductor:

B. RL Optimization Framework 

Figure 1 depicts the proposed optimization environment. At the beginning of each episode, a workload-aware PDN is loaded from the dataset and its SPICE netlist is parsed to extract the network topology and wire-width information. The topology remains fixed throughout the optimization process while the wire widths are modified by the agent. After an action is selected, the corresponding resistor values in the SPICE netlist are updated to reflect the new wire widths. The modified netlist is then analyzed using a circuit simulator to obtain node voltages and branch currents. These electrical quantities are used to evaluate the routing area, voltage-drop violations, and EM reliability of the current design. To enable efficient optimization, EM is estimated using the calibrated Black model described previously. The resulting area, voltage, and EM metrics are combined to construct the environment state and reward signal, which are returned to the agent for the next optimization step. This process is repeated until a termination condition is reached.

D. Deep Q-Network 

Finally, a Deep Q-Network (DQN) is used to approximate the action-value function Q(s, a ) with a neural network. This avoids explicitly storing a Q-table for the exponentially large PDN state space and enables generalization across similar wire-sizing configurations. The Q-network evaluates each wire using both local and global PDN information. For each wire, local features are concatenated with global features describing the overall PDN state. These inputs are processed by a shared multilayer perceptron consisting of two fully connected layers with 128 neurons and ReLU activations. The network then branches into two heads: a per-wire head that outputs Q-values for increasing or decreasing each wire width by ±∆w, and a global head that outputs the Q-value of the no-operation action. The proposed reinforcement learning framework was eval-uated on a dataset of workload-aware PDNs generated from 4-, 8-, and 16-core floorplans using PARSEC and SPLASH-2 workloads. The dataset contains both regular and irregular grid topologies under average and worst-case power scenarios. The DQN agent was trained on the generated PDNs and evaluated on unseen test instances. Performance was compared against the original workload-aware PDNs (Baseline) and a Simulated Annealing (SA) optimizer using the same objective and constraint definitions. Table I compares the proposed DQN approach against SA on the test set. Both methods satisfy all EM and voltage constraints, yielding reliability-compliant solutions. While SA achieves a slightly lower average normalized area (0.1438 ver-sus 0.1686), the difference in optimization quality is relatively small compared to the substantial difference in runtime. DQN completes optimization in an average of 171.99s, whereas SA requires 4561.67s, corresponding to an approximately 26 ×

speedup. Therefore, the proposed DQN approach offers a more favorable trade-off between optimization quality and compu-tational efficiency, achieving near-optimal area reduction at a fraction of the optimization cost. V. C ONCLUSIONS 

This paper presented a reinforcement learning-based frame-work for the optimization of workload-aware PDNs. The proposed methodology combines workload-dependent power distributions with a DQN agent that performs reliability-aware wire-width optimization under EM and IR-drop constraints. Experimental results on 4-, 8-, and 16-core PDNs demonstrate that the proposed approach reduces the average PDN area from 0.3170 to 0.1686, corresponding to a 47% reduction, while maintaining zero EM and voltage violations. Compared to simulated annealing, the DQN agent achieves comparable optimization quality while providing approximately 26 × faster optimization, reducing the average runtime from 4561.67s to 171.99s.
